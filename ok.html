Cygnus X-1

Detected in 1964 via suborbital X-ray sounding rockets and famous for the 1974 wager between Stephen Hawking and Kip Thorne, Cygnus X-1 was humanity's first confirmed black hole. Orbiting at just 0.2 AU from the blue supergiant star HDE 226868, it strips stellar wind into a 10-million-kelvin accretion disk spinning near the extremal Kerr limit.

Sagittarius A*

Anchoring the dynamical heart of our Milky Way galaxy in the constellation Sagittarius, Sgr A* was proven to be a supermassive singularity by tracking the extreme Keplerian orbits of the S-star cluster—earning Reinhard Genzel and Andrea Ghez the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics before the Event Horizon Telescope directly resolved its glowing shadow in 2022.

Galactic Distance 26,670 Light-Years
Event Horizon Diameter 25.4 Million km (0.17 AU)
Star S2 Pericenter Speed 7,650 km/s (2.55% c)
EHT Ring Angular Size 51.8 Microarcseconds

Because Sgr A* accretes via a radiatively inefficient advection-dominated flow (RIAF), its plasma flares shift rapidly on timescales of minutes.

Messier 87*

Located 53.5 million light-years away at the core of the supergiant elliptical galaxy Messier 87, M87* became the first black hole in history to have its shadow directly imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope in April 2019.

01 // 230 GHz VLBI INTERFEROMETRY

Relativistic Doppler Beaming Crescent

Plasma orbiting clockwise at ~0.5c beams intense synchrotron photons toward Earth on the southern rim of the 42-microarcsecond ring.

02 // BLANDFORD–ZNAJEK MAGNETOSPHERE

5,000-Light-Year Relativistic Jet

Ergospheric frame-dragging twists helical magnetic fields, collimating a superluminal plasma jet that escapes the host galaxy.

03 // SOLAR SYSTEM SCALE COMPARISON

256 AU Shadow Diameter

Its event horizon spans more than three times Pluto's orbital diameter and swallows ~0.1 solar masses per year.

// SECTOR 04 — HYPERLUMINOUS LYMAN-ALPHA QUASAR TON 618 • 66 BILLION M☉ • Rs ≈ 195 BILLION KM (1,300 AU)

TON 618

Observed at a cosmological redshift of z = 2.219 (10.4 billion years of light-travel time near the Canes Venatici–Coma Berenices border), TON 618 powers one of the brightest quasars in the known universe. Its super-heated accretion disk shines with the bolometric luminosity of 140 trillion Suns, outshining its entire host galaxy and ionizing a 320,000-light-year Lyman-alpha nebula.

// SECTOR 05 — STUPENDOUSLY LARGE BLACK HOLE (SLAB) PHOENIX A* • 100 BILLION M☉ • Rs ≈ 295 BILLION KM (1,975 AU)

Phoenix A*

Reigning at the core of the Phoenix Cluster 5.8 billion light-years away, Phoenix A* is the most massive singularity ever discovered (~100 billion M☉). A photon orbiting its photon sphere takes 22 days and 19 hours to complete a single lap. Because tidal forces scale inversely with mass squared ($M^{-2}$) at the horizon, an astronaut crossing Phoenix A*'s event horizon feels virtually zero spaghettification.
